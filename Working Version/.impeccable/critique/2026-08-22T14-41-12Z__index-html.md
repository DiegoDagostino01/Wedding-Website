## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**This does NOT read as AI-generated.** The hand-composed botanical SVG system (rose, daisy, delphinium, lilac, tulip, craspedia ball, sprig, leaf — species matched to the actual bouquet), the personal copy voice ("Kind to the lawns", "this is the one that catches people out"), the guest-picker RSVP, and the hand-illustrated journey map carry a deliberate human hand. No side-stripes, no gradient text, no glassmorphism, no identical card grids.

**Two tells persist, both minor:**

1. **20 em-dashes** in body copy. The detector flags this as an AI cadence tell — and in most contexts it is — but here the dashes serve the personal, conversational wedding voice ("and somehow, everything changed — and we can't wait"). A few could become other punctuation without losing warmth.

2. **Numbered section markers (01–04)** on the journey stage cards. These ARE legitimate — the journey is a real chronological sequence of four places, and the order carries information the reader needs (the story moves Swansea → Southampton → Iceland → Norway). This is the exact exception the Impeccable skill's ban permits: "One deliberate numbered sequence on one page is voice."

**Detector scan** (4 findings, all false positives or editorial style):
- `broken-image`: `<img id="galleryLightboxImage" alt="">` — JS-populated, not a bug
- `single-font`: "only font used is great vibes" — false positive from split-file scanning; the page actually loads Great Vibes + Montserrat + Noto Serif Display
- `em-dash-overuse`: 20 em-dashes — editorial style, see above
- `numbered-section-markers`: 01–04 in journey — legitimate sequential timeline

## Overall Impression

This page has come a long way since the first critique (31/40). The consistency score jumped from 2 to 4 after DESIGN.md alignment, duplicate CSS cleanup, and ARIA completion. The aesthetic score rose from 2 to 3 after cutting 8 redundant eyebrow labels — the page no longer reads as scaffolded. The footer contact line and fixed mailto links close real guest-experience gaps.

The single biggest opportunity now is the gallery lightbox — four beautiful photos with no actual lightbox targets. The JS opens a dialog with an empty `src=""` image. Whether the photos are coming later or the lightbox code needs wiring, a gallery section that can't show photos larger is a promise the page can't keep yet.

---

## What's Working

1. **RSVP UX writing is genuinely excellent.** Every state has clear, warm copy: "Please pick your name from the list" (specific, not blaming), "Can't find your name? Email us and we'll add you" (actionable), "Someone from your party has already replied" (clear next step), "Thank you — your reply is in" (confirms what happened). The guest picker prevents name typos; the decline path gracefully collapses the party section.

2. **The botanical identity is consistent and committed.** Every section divider is a unique sprig composition. The vine carries 41 blooms across 7 species, each with its own color token. The canopy and depth-floaters mirror the same botanical vocabulary. This is a visual system that would survive being stripped of all text and still be recognisable.

3. **The journey map is a genuinely ambitious, personal touch.** A hand-illustrated SVG map with scroll-drawn routes, custom marker illustrations for each city, ocean texture via SVG filters, and a Leaflet satellite fallback. It's the kind of feature a couple commissions, not the kind an AI scaffold produces.

## Priority Issues

### [P2] Gallery lightbox has no image targets
Four gallery wall items open a lightbox dialog with an empty `src=""`. The photos exist on disk (assets/Shadow-Photo.png, Proposal.png, Vinyard.png, Waterfall.png) but the lightbox isn't wired to display them. A guest clicks "Our Story" expecting to see it larger and gets an empty dialog.
**→ `/impeccable craft gallery-lightbox`** — wire the lightbox JS to load the corresponding full image from each gallery item's existing `<img>`.

### [P2] 20 em-dashes is an editorial cadence tell
Most are in the journey stage cards and story copy. A few could become periods, commas, or nothing without losing the warm, personal voice. Example: "somehow, everything changed — and we can't wait" → "somehow, everything changed. We can't wait." — same warmth, less AI-grammar cadence.
**→ `/impeccable quieter`** — reduce em-dashes from 20 to ~8, keeping them where they genuinely serve the conversational rhythm.

### [P3] Decorative load is deliberate but heavy
Eight fixed/viewport layers run simultaneously: vine (41 blooms), canopy (7 elements), depth-floaters (3), petal canvas, paper grain, garden-light wash, scroll-progress bar, and invitation veil (one-shot). Each is beautiful alone; together they're a sensory garden. The user chose to keep the overdrive canopy — this is the page's chosen maximalism. On low-end phones, the petal canvas + vine bloom transitions + canopy parallax may contend for the main thread.
**→ No command needed unless perf issues surface in real-world testing.**

### [P3] Countdown shows all four units regardless of distance
The quieter pass gated hours/minutes/seconds behind ≤7 days and ≤1 day, but all four unit boxes remain visible — guests 8 months out see "365 Days | – Hours | – Minutes | – Seconds." Three dashes in a row feels like a loading failure, not intentional design.
**→ `/impeccable quieter`** — hide hours/minutes/seconds units entirely (not just the values) when the wedding is more than 14 days away. Show only the Days unit.

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — The petal canvas + vine scroll handler + canopy parallax + depth-floaters + invitation veil all fire on scroll. On a mid-range phone, this could jank. The canopy and depth-floaters are gated behind `(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` which helps, but the vine and petals are not. No state persistence if the user switches tabs mid-RSVP (form data is lost). Touch targets at 44px were fixed in the adapt pass — good.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)** — The vine, canopy, depth-floaters, petals, paper grain, garden light, and scroll-progress are all `aria-hidden="true"` — correct. The journey map SVG has a proper `<title>` and `<desc>`. The gallery lightbox has `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal="true"`. But: the lightbox opens with an empty image — a screen reader user hears "Expanded photograph dialog" with no image content. The countdown shows "–" for hidden units, which screen readers will read as "dash" — confusing.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester)** — The RSVP form handles: empty guest name, invalid email, guest not in list, already-replied party member. But: what happens on mid-form refresh? State is lost. What happens if someone pastes a very long song request? No maxlength. What happens if the guest picker receives no results from the data fetch? The "not found" fallback shows, which is correct.

## Minor Observations

- Gallery images have `loading="lazy"` and `decoding="async"` — good, but the four gallery wall items should `fetchpriority="low"` to avoid competing with the critical hero image.
- The journey planner has a reassuring privacy note ("Nothing is stored — it's worked out in your browser") — excellent trust detail. Few wedding sites bother.
- The "Save the day to your calendar" button appears twice (once in event details, once in travel). This is legitimate — different contexts, same action. Not duplicate CTA intent.
- The invitation veil has a 3-second safety timeout — the page can never be stuck covered. Good defensive engineering.
- No `maxlength` on the RSVP message textarea — a guest could paste a novel.
